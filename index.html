Price Benchmarks

Published cloud prices are not comparable as printed. These pages normalise them — one unit, one hour basis, one currency — and put an adjustable total-cost scenario next to the headline rate.

S3-compatible buckets across 14 vendors, from S3 Standard to Glacier Deep Archive. Storage, egress, requests, retrieval, minimum durations and redundancy, with a scenario cost that recalculates from your own volumes.

Each benchmark is a folder in the repository, composed from a shared shell and its own dataset, markup and cost model. The maintenance guide documents how to add another.

What gets normalised

  1. The unit. Several vendors bill in binary units (1 GB = 2³⁰ bytes) while printing "GB"; others state no basis at all. Decimal versus binary is a 7.4 % difference on the same physical bytes.
  2. The month. Vendors that bill hourly extrapolate using 720, 730 or 744 hours, depending on who is doing the extrapolating. A 3 % spread on its own.
  3. What the headline excludes. Egress, requests and retrieval fees on the object side; provisioned IOPS, throughput and snapshots on the block side — which at AWS io2 can cost four times the capacity itself.

House rules

  1. One basis, stated on the page — 730 h/month, euros, excluding VAT, with the ECB reference rate and its date in the header. Every dataset carries the same rate.
  2. Physical units, not billing units — decimal-unit vendors are converted, so the comparison is per unit of what you actually get.
  3. Nothing is estimated — a charge a vendor does not publish reads not published, and a scenario total missing a component is prefixed ≥ rather than quietly treated as zero.
  4. Regions are labelled per row — where a vendor has no equivalent of the reference region, the row says which region it actually is.
  5. Capability gaps are not discounts — a vendor that cannot deliver the scenario is marked, and a product a vendor does not offer is absent, not free.
Maintained by someone who works at Exoscale; Exoscale's rows are highlighted in the tables. Figures come from each vendor's own published pages, all sourced at the bottom of each benchmark. Treat this as a starting point for your own verification, not as an authority — and note that list prices ignore the negotiated discounts and commitment terms where large deals are actually decided.
