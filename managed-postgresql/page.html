Managed PostgreSQL in Europe:
what a defined workload costs per month

Eleven providers sell managed PostgreSQL in or near Frankfurt, each in its own shape: plans with the disk inside, instances with storage billed apart, standby nodes billed as plans or as copies. This page prices the same workload at each of them. For every offer it picks the cheapest size that meets the vCPU, RAM, storage, standby nodes and point-in-time recovery you ask for, and shows what it costs per month. Every rate was read on 21 September 2026 from the vendors' own pricing pages, price feeds and APIs. Vendors sell sizes, not units, so this is a price per workload, not per GiB.

How it is priced

How the scenario is priced

  • One size per offer. Among the sizes with at least the requested vCPU and RAM per node, at least the requested standby nodes, and (unless switched on above) not burstable and not gated, the cheapest total wins. The size shown is that one.
  • Total = compute + storage + billed backup data. Compute is the hourly rate of all nodes times the hours. Storage is priced the way each vendor bills it, below. Backup data is zero unless you enter a figure, because how much backup a database generates is not published by anyone.
  • RAM is compared as stated, and strictly by default. Vendors write GB or GiB without always saying which; a 7 GB size is not accepted for an 8 GiB request, an 8 GB size is. Several vendors sell a "16" size with 15 GB (Aiven on AWS and OVHcloud, OVHcloud DB1-15): with the strict rule a 16 GiB request skips to the next size, which can double a row. The RAM rule above lets you accept sizes up to 10 % below; the size is then tagged with how far below it is.
  • A bar is hatched when the cheapest size still fails a requirement: no point-in-time recovery, a shorter window than asked, or a disk cap below the storage requested. Its price is what the vendor charges for the most it does offer. A missing capability is not a discount.
  • A price marked ≥ is a lower bound: a cost the vendor does not publish is missing from it.

Why the same words price differently

  • Storage is bundled or separate. Exoscale sells a plan with its disk inside and no way to add more (the API has a read-only disk size, no disk parameter on create or update), so a bigger disk means a bigger plan. Aiven sells the same plans but adds dynamic disk sizing on most clouds, up to five times the plan's disk, at a per-GB-hour rate; UpCloud and Hobbyist plans cannot be extended. AWS, Azure and Scaleway bill compute and storage apart. OVHcloud bundles a minimum disk per size (160 GiB on the 8 GB size) and bills extra beyond it per GB-hour and per node.
  • High availability is billed three ways. AWS Multi-AZ and Azure bill the instance and the storage twice. OVHcloud multiplies the per-node price by the nodes. Scaleway bills a second node without its management fee. Exoscale and Aiven price the two- and three-node plans as plans.
  • Point-in-time recovery is not one thing. AWS lets you choose 1 to 35 days on any size. Azure 7 to 35. Exoscale and Aiven fix it by plan tier (Exoscale 1, 2, 14 and 30 days). OVHcloud states a backup retention of 2, 14 and 30 days by plan.
  • Burstable and shared vCPU are off by default (AWS db.t*, Azure B-series). Their price is real but the performance is not comparable to a dedicated core.
